Microsoft Copilot for Swiss SMBs: what you actually need to know

Copilot is already in your Microsoft 365 subscription — sort of. This is the explanation I give clients before they buy anything: what the three Copilots are, what they cost in CHF, where the data goes, and how to roll it out without creating a compliance problem.

Every month someone asks me whether their company should "get Copilot". The honest answer is that they almost certainly already have one, probably don't need the expensive one for everybody, and should fix two things before switching it on for anyone. Let me unpack that.

There are three things called Copilot

1. Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat — included, free

Every Microsoft 365 business user already has Copilot Chat: a ChatGPT-style assistant at m365.cloud.microsoft, in the Edge sidebar and in the Microsoft 365 app. It is grounded in the web, not in your company data, but it runs with enterprise data protection: your prompts are not used to train models and are covered by your Microsoft 365 contract. For drafting, summarising a pasted document, or explaining an Excel formula, it does 80% of what most people want from AI at work — for nothing.

2. Microsoft 365 Copilot — the licensed one

This is Copilot inside Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams and PowerPoint, grounded in your emails, files, chats and meetings via Microsoft Graph. "Summarise what I missed in this Teams thread", "draft a reply based on the attached contract", "build a slide deck from this Word document". This is the one that costs money and the one that raises the compliance questions.

3. Copilot Cowork — agents, billed by usage

Cowork is the agent layer that carries out multi-step tasks and is billed on consumption rather than per seat. I wrote about the pricing change here. For most SMBs it is a later conversation; this article is about the first two.

What it costs in Switzerland

Since 1 July 2026 Microsoft sells Copilot to small businesses mainly as bundles, and in CHF the bundle is clearly cheaper than buying the base plan plus the add-on. Microsoft Switzerland list prices, per user per month, annual commitment, excluding MwSt:

  • Microsoft 365 Business Standard + Copilot Business — CHF 19.03 (Standard alone is CHF 11.76)
  • Microsoft 365 Business Premium + Copilot Business — CHF 25.92 (Premium alone is CHF 20.69)
  • Copilot Business add-on on an existing Business plan — CHF 17.00 list; promotional CHF 14.57 for the first year if bought before 31 December 2026
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot for E3/E5 tenants — enterprise add-on, priced on request

The maths that matters: moving one user from Business Premium to Premium + Copilot costs CHF 5.23 more per month. Moving a user from Standard to Standard + Copilot costs CHF 7.27 more. If that user saves fifteen minutes a week, it has paid for itself. The question is not whether Copilot is "worth it" in general — it is which users save that time. Usually not all of them.

Where your data goes

This is the question Swiss clients ask first, rightly. The short version:

  • Your prompts and Copilot's responses are stored in each user's Exchange Online mailbox, so they follow your tenant's data location. If your tenant is in Switzerland, they are in Switzerland. (If you don't know where your tenant is, read this first.)
  • The AI processing itself runs on Microsoft's Azure OpenAI capacity. For Swiss and European tenants Microsoft commits to processing within its EU Data Boundary, with limited exceptions it documents. It is not guaranteed to be in Switzerland. Check Microsoft's current documentation before you make statements to clients.
  • Training: your data is not used to train the underlying models. That is contractual, not a setting you can get wrong.
  • CLOUD Act: as with all of Microsoft 365, US jurisdiction applies regardless of data location. If that is unacceptable for a particular data set, the answer is not to use Copilot on that data — and probably not to keep it in Microsoft 365 either.

The nDSG angle nobody mentions in the sales deck

Copilot does not give users access to anything new. It gives them a very good search engine over everything they already have access to. In most SMB tenants that is far more than anyone realises: the HR folder shared to "Everyone" in 2019, the salary spreadsheet in a Teams channel, the client list in someone's OneDrive shared with the whole company. Before Copilot, nobody found those because nobody looked. After Copilot, "what does Hans earn?" gets an answer.

Under the nDSG you are responsible for personal data being accessible only to those who need it. Copilot does not create that problem, but it makes an existing one visible — fast. So the two things to do before switching it on for anyone:

1. Fix oversharing

Run the SharePoint sharing reports, look at every site shared with "Everyone" or "Everyone except external users", and close down what shouldn't be open. If that's too much to tackle at once, Restricted SharePoint Search lets you limit Copilot to an allow-list of sites while you clean up.

2. Label what is sensitive

Create two or three sensitivity labels (Internal, Confidential, Personal data) and apply them to the obvious libraries. Copilot respects labels: it will not summarise a Confidential document into an email to the wrong person, and it tells the user why.

Then write a one-page acceptable-use note. Not a policy document — a page. What Copilot may be used for, what must be checked by a human before it leaves the building, and that client data does not go into non-Microsoft AI tools. The nDSG does not mention AI, but it does expect you to have thought about this.

A realistic rollout for a 10–50 person Swiss firm

  • Month 0: everyone gets a 30-minute session on Copilot Chat — the free one. Most of the productivity gain is here, and it is free.
  • Month 1: fix sharing and labels as above. Honestly, do this even if you never buy Copilot.
  • Month 2–4: pilot the licensed Copilot with five to ten people who write and summarise a lot — management, sales, anyone drowning in email and meetings. Not the whole company.
  • Month 4: ask the pilot group one question: would you give it back? Keep the licences for those who say no, drop the rest. In my experience about half keep it, and the ones who do are emphatic.

What I'd tell you if you asked me over coffee

Start with the free one. Fix your sharing. Pilot the paid one with the people who will actually use it. Don't buy it for the whole company because a reseller suggested it, and don't avoid it because of a headline about AI and data — the data question has a clear, checkable answer for Swiss tenants.
